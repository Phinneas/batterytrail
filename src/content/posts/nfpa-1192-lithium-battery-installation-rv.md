NFPA 1192 does not spell out lithium battery installation details itself. It defers electrical requirements to NFPA 70 for AC systems and to ANSI/RVIA LV for DC systems, and it's ANSI/RVIA LV that requires any lithium battery installed in an RV to be "listed," meaning it carries an independent safety certification such as UL 1973 or UL 2054.

## What NFPA 1192 Actually Covers

NFPA 1192, Standard on Recreational Vehicles, covers fire and life safety criteria across the entire RV: structural integrity, plumbing, fuel-burning appliances, vehicular systems, and electrical systems. Its scope statement is broad by design, since it exists to set uniform minimum safety standards across an industry where no single manufacturer previously followed the same rulebook.

What it doesn't do is function as a standalone electrical code. For AC electrical systems, NFPA 1192 points directly to NFPA 70 (the National Electrical Code), specifically Article 551, which governs RVs and RV parks. For DC electrical systems, including battery banks, it points to ANSI/RVIA LV, the RV Industry Association's own Low Voltage standard. If you're looking for the specific paragraph in NFPA 1192 that tells you how to wire a lithium battery bank, you won't find it. That level of detail lives one layer down, in ANSI/RVIA LV.

## Where the Battery Requirements Really Live

ANSI/RVIA LV is the document doing the actual work for lithium battery installations. Its 2020 edition added new sections addressing solar prep installations and lithium battery systems directly, including a requirement that lithium batteries be "listed," the same terminology used throughout the National Electrical Code to mean certified by a nationally recognized testing laboratory (NRTL) such as UL.

In practice, this means a lithium battery installed in an RV built to code needs a UL listing, most commonly UL 1973 for the complete battery system or UL 2054 for household and commercial batteries. A battery that only carries a UN 38.3 transport certification, with no UL listing, does not meet this bar. RVIA member manufacturers are required to build to the current edition of both NFPA 1192 and ANSI/RVIA LV, and RVIA has required members to keep a physical copy of each new edition on site since the 2021 update took effect on July 1, 2021.

## What NFPA Is Still Working On

Lithium battery installation guidance in RVs is still evolving, and NFPA has said so directly. NFPA convened a Lithium-Ion and RV Life Safety Task Group, with representatives from Thor Industries, Forest River, Grand Design, RVIA, KOA, and independent fire consultants, to study fire and life safety issues specific to lithium batteries in RVs and campgrounds. The group met in February and August of 2024. Its report to the full NFPA 1192/1194 technical committee stated plainly that, given how quickly lithium battery technology is changing, the group had no formal recommendations to make at that time.

The task group did flag two areas for future rulemaking: metal enclosures for battery compartments and dedicated fire detection or alarm systems for those compartments. Neither is currently a hard requirement under NFPA 1192 or ANSI/RVIA LV. If you're installing a lithium battery today, code compliance stops at the "listed" battery requirement. Enclosure material and fire detection are best practices under active discussion, not yet enforceable code.

## What This Means If You're Installing Your Own Battery

If you're a DIY RV owner replacing a battery rather than a manufacturer building to code, none of this is legally binding on you the way it is on RVIA members. But it's still the clearest available benchmark for what a professionally built, code-compliant system looks like, and it matters for two practical reasons. First, insurance and warranty claims can hinge on whether an installation matches recognized standards. Second, a UL-listed battery has already been through the thermal runaway, short circuit, and crush testing that ANSI/RVIA LV is designed to require, so choosing one gets you most of the way to a code-equivalent installation even without a permit or inspection.

## NFPA 1192 vs. ANSI/RVIA LV vs. NFPA 70

| Document | What it governs | Lithium-specific requirement |
|---|---|---|
| NFPA 1192 | Overall RV fire and life safety scope | None directly, defers to the two documents below |
| NFPA 70 (Article 551) | AC electrical systems in RVs | General electrical safety, not lithium-specific |
| ANSI/RVIA LV | DC electrical systems in RVs | Lithium batteries must be "listed" (UL 1973, UL 2054) |
