Buy a hardshell if you camp often or cover long highway miles. Buy a soft shell rooftop tent if sleeping space per dollar is the priority. That's the whole decision, and everything else is detail.

The gap is real in both directions. A hardshell opens in 30 to 60 seconds and sits lower on the roof. Neither type wins outright, and the specs below show where each one pulls ahead. I compare the two on setup time, weight, roof load, sleeping capacity and cost, then flag the cases where the cheaper tent is the better buy.

## One-Paragraph Verdict: Which Rooftop Tent Type Actually Wins

Buy a hardshell if you camp often or cover long highway miles. Buy a soft shell rooftop tent if sleeping space per dollar is the priority. That's the whole decision, and everything else is detail.

The hardshell case rests on speed and weather. Hardshells win on setup speed (30 to 90 seconds) and aerodynamics at highway speeds, per Everlead Outdoor. A folded hardshell often sits just 6 to 8 inches tall, so it creates minimal drag and wind noise according to Rooftop Tent Lab. A softshell needs 3 to 10 minutes once you factor in the rain fly and poles, and the taller closed profile contributes to the commonly reported 10 to 25 percent highway fuel economy drop.

The softshell case rests on room and money. Outdoor Gear Lab notes quality softshells typically cost half as much as hardshell models while delivering the same core benefits: a thick mattress, weather protection, and elevation off cold, uneven ground. The price gap is convenience, not comfort.

My take goes against the usual forum advice to just buy once and cry once. I would skip the premium hardshell unless you're actually touring, because a weekend camper pays for setup speed they use twice a month. If your trips are occasional, put the savings into a quality softshell and better bedding. If you camp weekly, the hardshell wins on the morning you're packing up in rain.

## When a Hardshell Rooftop Tent Wins

I recommend a hardshell rooftop tent when setup speed and long term maintenance are top priorities. A clamshell design, such as the Roofnest Falcon 3 EVO Air, opens in about 30 seconds, requiring only the release of two latches and the action of gas struts, which contrasts with the 3 to 10 minutes a fold-out softshell requires once you account for the rain fly and poles.

### Durability and Driving Efficiency

Hardshell designs resist weather better and last longer. The waterproof shell handles storms and ages well, while softshell ripstop covers need more frequent cleaning for mildew. Driving economics favor hardshells too. Their low profile, often just 6 to 8 inches tall when closed, creates minimal drag and wind noise. That matters because owners commonly report a 10 to 25% drop in highway fuel economy with a rooftop tent mounted, which gets worse with the taller softshell profile.

### Practicality and Gear Management

I think integrated features make hardshells the better call for frequent campers. Many units come with exterior crossbars, so you don't lose rooftop carrying capacity. I haven't found a softshell that supports these bars. You also skip the chore of ground clearing and stowing a separate travel cover, which saves the 20 minutes of clearing rocks and sticks before pitching.

### Considering Weight

If weight worries you, look at specific models like the Roofnest Falcon 3 EVO Air. It's cited as being about 130 pounds, making it one of the lighter hardshell options mentioned.

## FAQ

### Are soft shell roof top tents any good?

Yes, for the money they're the best value in rooftop camping. Outdoor Gear Lab reports that "quality softshells typically cost half as much as hardshell models while delivering the exact same core benefits: a thick mattress, bombproof weather protection, and elevation off cold, uneven ground." The trade-offs are real. Setup and teardown take 5 to 10 minutes, and the zippered travel cover ages worse than a hard shell. I would still buy one if you camp a few weekends a season and want the most sleeping space per dollar.

### What is the best softshell rooftop tent?

Switchback Travel names the Thule Approach its Best Softshell Rooftop Tent, and the Approach Medium is listed at 128 lb with a 33.6 sq. Ft. Floor and 2, 2+, and 3+ capacities. That weight is the lightest softshell in its comparison table. The Smittybilt Gen2 Overlander is the budget pick at 154 lb with a 35.1 sq. Ft. Floor, and Switchback calls it "our favorite budget rooftop tent." My pick between them is the Thule Approach if your rack capacity is tight, and the Smittybilt if floor area matters more.

### What is the cheapest hardshell rooftop tent?

The cheapest hardshell in the Switchback Travel table is the Thule Widesky at 147 lb with a 30 sq. Ft. Floor, and it's the only hardshell there that undercuts the priciest softshells. If your budget is the hard limit, I wouldn't stretch for a hardshell. You get sleeping space for less with a softshell, and that's the honest trade.

### Is there a hard shell rooftop tent with bottom access?

No mainstream hardshell in the sources I checked offers bottom access, and I wouldn't expect one. Hardshells open from the top. As rooftoptentlab describes it, a clamshell "pops open in about 30 seconds: release two latches and gas struts do the rest." Softshells unfold over the side on a ladder, which is closer to what people mean by bottom access. If climbing through a hatch or ladder opening matters most, look at a fold-out design instead.

### Is there truly ANY benefit to having hard shell vs soft shell tent? I can't imagine paying an extra $2k just for a plastic cover.?

Yes, and the gap is bigger than the cover. Everlead Outdoor reports hard shells win on "setup speed (30-90 seconds), aerodynamics at highway speeds, all-weather durability, and long-term maintenance cost." A softshell sits 10 to 14 inches tall closed versus 6 to 9 inches for a hardshell, and owners commonly report a 10 to 25% drop in highway fuel economy with a rooftop tent mounted. Camp twice a year? I would skip the hardshell. The common advice to always buy hard shell ignores how little you use it.
