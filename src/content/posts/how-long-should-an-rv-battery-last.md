An AGM RV battery lasts 4–7 years, or roughly 300 to 700 cycles at 50% depth of discharge. A LiFePO4 (lithium) battery lasts 10+ years, or 3,000 to 5,000 cycles at 80% DoD depending on how deeply you discharge it.

## AGM Battery Lifespan

Under normal RV use (seasonal camping, not daily cycling), an AGM battery gives 4–7 years of service. Cycle life runs 300 to 700 full cycles at 50% DoD before capacity falls to about 80% of rated.

Three things cut that short:

- **Overcharging.** Sustained charging above 14.8V drives gassing and electrolyte loss. AGM cells are sealed, so that water never comes back.
- **Chronic partial charge.** Leaving an AGM sitting below 12.6V lets sulfate crystals harden on the plates. This is the single most common cause of premature RV battery death.
- **Freezing while discharged.** A fully charged lead-acid battery's electrolyte doesn't freeze until roughly -80°F. A fully discharged one freezes at about **+20°F**, and at 40% state of charge it freezes near -16°F. The ice expands, bends the plates, and often cracks the case.

## LiFePO4 Battery Lifespan

Expect 10+ years. Cycle ratings vary by brand, and the number depends heavily on how deep you discharge:

- **Battle Born** rates its 100Ah 12V LiFePO4 at **3,000–5,000 cycles**, backed by a 10-year warranty.
- **LiTime** publishes a depth-of-discharge ladder for its 12V 100Ah: **4,000 cycles at 100% DoD, 6,000 at 80% DoD, and 15,000 at 60% DoD**, with a 5-year warranty.

Put that against real use. Cycling once per weekend is about 52 cycles a year. At 4,000 cycles, that's 76 years of cycling, far beyond any realistic ownership window. For weekend RVers, **calendar aging (10–15 years) is the practical limit, not cycle count.** Cycle ratings only become the binding constraint for full-timers cycling daily.

Note the trade-off buried in LiTime's ladder: discharging to 60% instead of 100% nearly quadruples cycle life. If you size your bank so you rarely go below 40% remaining, the pack outlives the RV.

## What Shortens RV Battery Life

**Heat.** This is the most underrated killer. Every 15°F (8°C) of sustained temperature above 77°F roughly halves lead-acid service life. A battery rated for 10 years at 77°F becomes a 5-year battery at 92°F and a 30-month battery at 106°F. A battery bay on the sunny side of an RV parked in Arizona is a battery bay eating years off your investment. Heat damage is permanent. Capacity never comes back.

**Sitting discharged.** Both chemistries suffer, for different reasons. AGM sulfates. LiFePO4 cells can drop below the BMS cutoff (around 10V for a 12V pack) and refuse to wake up. Before storing, charge AGM to 100% and LiFePO4 to 60–80%.

**Storage self-discharge.** A lead-acid battery stored at 80°F self-discharges about 4% per week. Stored at 65°F, that falls to roughly 3% per month. Cool storage buys you months.

## Signs Your Battery Is Near End of Life

Age alone isn't a verdict. A 6-year-old AGM that still passes a load test is a working battery, and a 2-year-old one that's been cooked in a hot bay may already be finished. Test before you replace.
