## Executive Summary

Solid-state battery electrolytes are the enabling materials for batteries that replace the flammable liquid electrolyte of today's lithium-ion cells with a solid ion conductor, in principle allowing lithium-metal anodes and higher energy density. Four-plus material families compete: **sulfide** (fastest, Li10GeP2S12-class conductivities of 12–25 mS/cm at room temperature [1,2], moisture-intolerant [6]), **oxide** (garnet LLZO, NASICON LATP; stable but brittle and hard to process [18,19]), **halide** (a 2020s resurgence; Li3InCl6 at ~1.5–2 mS/cm [37,39], mixed-anion halides at 11 mS/cm as of 2024 [40,41], oxidation-stable to ~4.3 V [42,43]), and **polymer** (processable and flexible but ~10⁻⁴ S/cm or worse at room temperature [70]; PEO only practical at 60–80 °C [49,50]). Thin-film LiPON occupies the microbattery niche [60,61,62].

Key findings from this research round:

1. **Conductivity is no longer the bottleneck for the leading classes** — best sulfides and halides now match or exceed liquid-electrolyte conductivity (~10 mS/cm) [66,40,41]. The bottlenecks have moved to interfaces, mechanical behavior, and manufacturing [106,94].
2. **Interfaces dominate the failure story:** void formation on stripping [98,99], lithium dendrite penetration (the "dendrite-free" premise has been falsified [107,108]), narrow electrochemical stability windows requiring coatings and interlayers [65,11], and moisture sensitivity (H2S from sulfides [7,8,9]; dry-room dew points below −45 °C [114]).
3. **Demonstrated solid-state cells sit at ~250–400 Wh/kg (stack/lab level) [89,90],** roughly at or modestly above today's best commercial Li-ion (260–300 Wh/kg class) [87], with a >500 Wh/kg cell-level claim reported for an all-solid-state lithium–sulfur cell [91,92] and a >400 Wh/kg silicon-anode cell demonstrated [90]; pack-level economics remain unproven [94].
4. **As of mid-2026, no full-solid-state *passenger-car* battery was found on sale in this research round (survey inference — the cited sources document samples, prototypes, and pilot lines, not a market-wide audit) [123].** Every mass-produced or road-tested "solid-state" passenger-car product (NIO/WeLion 150 kWh pack — production discontinued Nov 2025 per NIO's CEO, via Electrive [126], CATL condensed [155,209], Mercedes/Stellantis Factorial prototypes [124,125]) is semi-solid/quasi-solid. The standing exception outside passenger cars: Blue Solutions' polymer LMP bus battery, in production since 2011 [194]. Full-solid-state passenger-car launches cluster around 2027–2030 (Toyota [142,143], Samsung SDI [149,151], Nissan [178,180], CATL/BYD small batches [159,119]; Gotion 2030 [163,167]; SK On 2029 [170]), with pilot lines operating and B-samples shipping to OEMs [123,128,137].
5. **Forecasts and economics disagree sharply** — analyst projections for 2030 global shipments range from ~509 GWh (CITIC; full-solid-state scope) to 808 GWh (CICC; including >650 GWh semi-solid) [219,220] — the spread partly reflects differing scope, not pure disagreement; cost-parity timing ranges from "this decade" (BYD's claimed $70/kWh goal [119]; single source, low-medium confidence) to "not this decade" (CRU [118]); and even the energy-density promise is contested by cell-level models that put practical garnet pouch cells at only ~272 Wh/kg [94].

## 1. Material classes and their trade-offs

### 1.1 Sulfide electrolytes

Sulfides hold the conductivity record among lithium solid electrolytes. LGPS (Li10GeP2S12) was reported at **12 mS/cm at room temperature** — described at the time as exceeding liquid organic electrolytes [1: Kamaya, N. et al., "A lithium superionic conductor," Nature Materials 10, 682–686 (2011) — https://www.nature.com/articles/nmat3066]; a 2016 derivative, Li9.54Si1.74P1.44S11.7Cl0.3, reached **25 mS/cm** [2: Kato, Y. et al., "High-power all-solid-state batteries using sulfide superionic conductors," Nature Energy 1, 16030 (2016) — https://doi.org/10.1038/nenergy.2016.30]. LGPS-type frameworks conduct in 3D, not 1D [3: Mo, Y., Ong, S. P., Ceder, G., "First Principles Study of the Li10GeP2S12 Lithium Super Ionic Conductor Material," Chem. Mater. 24, 15–17 (2012) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/cm203303y]. A secondary review cites a further composition at 32 mS/cm, but the primary source was not verified in this round — treat as inferred-from-review [4: Adv. Mater. 2025 review — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12783987/].

Argyrodites (Li6PS5X, X = Cl, Br, I) are the most practical sulfide workhorse: ~1 mS/cm class as-synthesized, tunable toward ~10 mS/cm [5: "Complex Dynamics in Argyrodite Solid-State Ion Conductors," PMC13084999 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13084999/]; dry-milled Li6PS5Cl reports ~2.4–2.8 mS/cm at 25 °C versus 1.0–1.9 mS/cm for wet-milled variants [6: "Ionic and Electronic Conductivities of Lithium Argyrodite Li6PS5Cl Electrolytes Prepared via Wet Milling and Post-Annealing," Frontiers in Chemistry 9, 778057 (2021) — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/chemistry/articles/10.3389/fchem.2021.778057/full]. They are ductile (cold-pressable, no sintering), cheap-precursor, but moisture-sensitive: exposure releases toxic **H2S** [6][7: Moisture Stability of Sulfide Solid-State Electrolytes, Frontiers in Energy Research 10, 882508 (2022) — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/energy-research/articles/10.3389/fenrg.2022.882508/full][8: Towards a Practical Use of Sulfide Solid Electrolytes…: Impact of Dry Room Exposure on H2S Release and Material Properties, Batteries & Supercaps 7, e202300380 (2023) — https://chemistry-europe.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/batt.202300380][9: Investigating the Production Atmosphere for Sulfide-Based Electrolyte Layers Regarding Occupational Health and Safety, Batteries 9, 472 (2023) — https://www.mdpi.com/2313-0105/9/9/472]. Nanoporous β-Li3PS4 shows ~1000× conductivity enhancement over bulk with a claimed wide window [10: Liu, Z. et al., "Anomalous High Ionic Conductivity of Nanoporous β-Li3PS4," J. Am. Chem. Soc. 135, 975–978 (2013) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/ja3110895].

**Stability is the fatal weakness.** DFT windows: LGPS ≈1.6–2.5 V vs Li depending on the study (reduction ~1.6–1.7 V; oxidation ~2.1 V per [3][65] or 2–2.5 V per [11]) [3][11][65]; argyrodites ~2.0–2.5 V vs Li [12: TU Delft doctoral thesis, "Probing Li-ion transport in Sulfide-based solid-state batteries" — https://doi.org/10.4233/uuid:4c1fca32-534f-464b-9035-a6a622ca1679]. Against Li metal, LGPS decomposes to Li3P/Li2S/LixGe, forming a high-impedance interphase [13: "Differentiating chemical and electrochemical degradation of lithium germanium thiophosphate and the role of atomic layer deposited protection layers," Materials Advances (RSC) (2022) — https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2022/ma/d2ma00776b]; at the cathode, LGPS|NCM degrades above ~3.7 V with oxygen release at high state of charge [14: "A mechanistic investigation of the Li10GeP2S12|LiNi1-x-yCoxMnyO2 interface stability in all-solid-state lithium batteries," Nature Communications 12, 6208 (2021) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-26895-4]. CV-based "up to 7 V" claims are kinetic artifacts [12][11]. Experimental windows overestimate thermodynamic stability because decomposition kinetics at inert contacts are slow [15: IOP ECS Meeting Abstract, "Electrochemical Stability of Li10GeP2S12 and Li7La3Zr2O12 Solid Electrolytes," MA2016-03/2/663 — https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1149/MA2016-03/2/663].

### 1.2 Oxide electrolytes

**Garnet LLZO (Li7La3Zr2O12)** is the most-studied oxide: doped variants reach ~1 mS/cm at room temperature (e.g., Te-doped 1.02×10⁻³ S/cm at 30 °C [16: "Lithium ion transport properties of high conductive tellurium substituted Li7La3Zr2O12 cubic lithium garnets," J. Power Sources (2013) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378775313005600]; Al/Ta co-doped 4.59×10⁻⁴ S/cm at 20 °C [17: "Enhancing the ionic conductivity and stabilizing cubic structure of garnet-type Li6.25-xAl0.25La3Zr2-xTaxO12 by Al/Ta co-doping," J. Solid State Chem. (2020) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022459620307805]), but densification needs sintering typically **>1200 °C** [18: "Research Progress and Prospect of Solid Electrolyte...," MDPI (2026), 2304-6740/14/6/148 — https://www.mdpi.com/2304-6740/14/6/148][19: Development of highly dense Ga-LLZO solid electrolyte pallet for All-Solid-State Battery using Machine Learning Approach, Solid State Communications (2025) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0038109825002133][20: Investigating the Calcination and Sintering of Li7La3Zr2O12 (LLZO) Solid Electrolytes, Chem. Mater. 33 (2021) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.chemmater.0c04393]. LLZO is not thermodynamically inert toward Li: DFT shows Zr reduction, dopant-dependent (Ta < Nb < Al) [21: Zhu, Y., He, X., Mo, Y., "Dopant-Dependent Stability of Garnet Solid Electrolyte Interfaces with Lithium Metal," Adv. Energy Mater. 9, 1803440 (2019) — https://doi.org/10.1002/aenm.201803440]; air exposure forms Li2CO3, which raises interfacial resistance and degrades Li wetting [22: "The origin of high electrolyte–electrode interfacial resistances in lithium cells containing garnet type solid electrolytes," Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys. 16, 18294 (2014) — https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2014/cp/c4cp02921f][23: "Impact of air exposure and surface chemistry on Li–Li7La3Zr2O12 interfacial resistance," J. Mater. Chem. A (2017) — https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2017/ta/c7ta03162a][24: "Li2CO3-affiliative mechanism for air-accessible interface engineering of garnet electrolyte via facile liquid metal painting," Nature Communications 11, 3802 (2020) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-17493-x][25: Dynamical evolution of CO2 and H2O on garnet electrolyte elucidated by ambient pressure X-ray spectroscopies, Nat. Commun. 15, 3231 (2024) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-47071-4]. Experimentally, Al-doped LLZO oxidizes at ~4.3 V vs Li at 80 °C — kinetically tolerant of high-voltage cathodes in practice [26: "Electrochemical Oxidation in Garnet-Type Solid Electrolyte by Formation of Point Defects" (2025), OSTI records — https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/2587336]. DFT puts LLZO's thermodynamic oxidation limit at 2.68–2.91 V vs Li (2.68 V [65]; 2.91 V in Han et al. 2016 [11]) — i.e., the observed ~4.3 V window is a kinetic, not thermodynamic, tolerance.

**NASICON LATP** reached 7×10⁻⁴ S/cm at 298 K in the original report [27: Aono, H. et al., "Ionic Conductivity of Solid Electrolytes Based on Lithium Titanium Phosphate," J. Electrochem. Soc. 137, 1023 (1990) — https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1149/1.2086597], with ~10⁻³ S/cm claimed later [28: "Polycationic doping of the LATP ceramic electrolyte for Li-ion batteries," RSC Advances 12 (2022) — https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2022/ra/d2ra05782d][29: "Reduced Sintering Temperatures of Li+ Conductive Li1.3Al0.3Ti1.7(PO4)3 Ceramics," Crystals 10(5), 408 (2020) — https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4352/10/5/408]; it is cheap and air-stable, but **Ti⁴⁺→Ti³⁺ reduction** makes it unstable against Li metal, degrading through a mixed-conducting interphase [28][30: "Interfacial lithiation of lithium aluminum titanium phosphate explored by 7Li NMR," Communications Chemistry 8 (2025) — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s42004-025-01505-2][31: "Stabilizing the Interface of NASICON Solid Electrolyte against Li Metal with Atomic Layer Deposition," ACS Appl. Mater. Interfaces 10 (2018) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/full/10.1021/acsami.8b06366][32: "Chemomechanical Failure Mechanism Study in NASICON-Type Li1.3Al0.3Ti1.7(PO4)3 Solid-State Lithium Batteries," Chem. Mater. 32 (2020) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/acs.chemmater.9b05295]. Perovskite **LLTO** has ~10⁻³ S/cm bulk-grain conductivity but is grain-boundary-limited to ~10⁻⁵ S/cm polycrystalline [33: Inaguma, Y. et al., "High ionic conductivity in lithium lanthanum titanate," Solid State Communications 86, 689–693 (1993) — https://oamonitor.ireland.openaire.eu/national/search/publication?pid=10.1016%2F0038-1098%2893%2990841-a][34: Stramare, S., Thangadurai, V., Weppner, W., "Lithium Lanthanum Titanates: A Review," Chem. Mater. 15, 3974–3990 (2003) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/cm0300516][35: "Elucidating the nature of grain boundary resistance in lithium lanthanum titanate," J. Mater. Chem. A 9 (2021) — https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2021/ta/d0ta11539h]; its Ti redox gives the same Li-metal instability (inference from shared Ti chemistry — not separately verified) [materials-gaps].

### 1.3 Halide electrolytes

The 2018–2019 revival made halides a contender: cold-pressed **Li3YCl6/Li3YBr6** exceed 1 mS/cm with 94% coulombic efficiency against LiCoO2 without cathode coatings [36: Asano, T. et al., "Solid Halide Electrolytes with High Lithium-Ion Conductivity for Application in 4 V Class Bulk-Type All-Solid-State Batteries," Adv. Mater. 30, 1803075 (2018) — https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.201803075]; **Li3InCl6** reached 1.49×10⁻³ S/cm at 25 °C, is air-stable, water-synthesizable, and stable against oxide cathodes [37: Li, X. et al., "Air-stable Li3InCl6 electrolyte with high voltage compatibility for all-solid-state batteries," Energy Environ. Sci. 12, 2665–2671 (2019) — https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2019/ee/c9ee02311a][38: "The Effect of Phosphoric Acid on the Preparation of High-Performance Li3InCl6 Solid-State Electrolytes by Water-Mediated Synthesis," Materials 18(9), 2077 (2025) — https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/18/9/2077][39: Li, X. et al., Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 58, 16427–16432 (2019) — https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.201909805]. Mixed-anion Li3MX6 halides reached **11 mS/cm at room temperature** in 2024 via collective anion motion — the first halide class to hit liquid-comparable values [40: Liu et al., Nat. Chem. 16, 1584–1591 (2024) — https://doi.org/10.1038/s41557-024-01634-6][41: UMD news, Oct 31, 2024 — https://energy.umd.edu/news/story/advanced-solid-electrolytes-break-world-record-for-ionic-conductivity].

Halides are oxidation-stable to ~4.3 V vs Li (chlorides) [42: "Material Design Strategy for Halide Solid Electrolytes Li3MX6 (X = Cl, Br, and I) for All-Solid-State High-Voltage Li-Ion Batteries," Chem. Mater. (2021) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/full/10.1021/acs.chemmater.1c00555][43: "A review of solid-state halide electrolyte matched LiCoO2 and Ni-rich NCM," J. Phys.: Conf. Ser. 2459, 012026 (2023) — https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1742-6596/2459/1/012026], but **reduction-unstable against Li metal**: they form a reaction layer on direct contact, requiring buffer layers or In–Li alloy anodes [44: "Lithium-Metal Anode Instability of the Superionic Halide Solid Electrolytes and the Implications for Solid-State Batteries," Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 60 (2021) — https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.202015238][45: "A kinetically stable anode interface for Li3YCl6-based all-solid-state lithium batteries," J. Mater. Chem. A 9 (2021) — https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2021/ta/d1ta03042f][46: "Revealing Dynamic Evolution of the Anode-Electrolyte Interphase in All-Solid-State Batteries with Excellent Cyclability" (2024, Ceder group) — https://ceder.berkeley.edu/publications/2024_SeYoung_Interphase.pdf]. Moisture tolerance is intermediate and material-specific [47: Investigating Dry Room Compatibility of Chloride Solid-State Electrolytes for Scalable Manufacturing, J. Electrochem. Soc. 170, 080534 (2023) — https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1149/1945-7111/acee24][48: Halide solid-state electrolytes for all-solid-state batteries: structural design, synthesis, environmental stability, interface optimization and challenges, Chem. Sci. 14, 10741 (2023) — https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2023/sc/d3sc02093b].

### 1.4 Polymer and composite electrolytes

PEO-based electrolytes are the benchmark polymer: excellent processability and safety, but room-temperature conductivity of 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁶ S/cm, reaching the practical 10⁻³ S/cm range only at 60–80 °C [49: "Research Progress and Application of PEO-Based Solid State Electrolytes," Frontiers in Energy Research 9, 726738 (2021) — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/energy-research/articles/10.3389/fenrg.2021.726738/full][50: "Enhancing the Lithium Ion Conductivity of an All Solid-State Electrolyte via Dry and Solvent-Free Scalable Series Production Processes," J. Electrochem. Soc. 167, 020558 (2020) — https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1149/1945-7111/ab6f77/pdf]. PEO's oxidation limit (~3.9 V vs Li) excludes 4 V-class cathodes without modification [51: "A Comprehensive Review of Sulfide Solid-State Electrolytes: Properties, Synthesis, Applications, and Challenges," Crystals 15(6), 492 (2025) — https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4352/15/6/492]. Single-ion polymers trade conductivity for transference number (~10⁻⁵–10⁻⁴ S/cm at 60 °C) [52: "Single-Ion Lithium Conducting Polymers with High Ionic Conductivity...," PMC9306921 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9306921/][53: "Self-Healing Single-Ion Conducting Polymer Electrolyte Formed via Supramolecular Networks for Lithium Metal Batteries," ACS collections — https://acs.figshare.com/collections/Self-Healing_Single-Ion_Conducting_Polymer_Electrolyte_Formed_via_Supramolecular_Networks_for_Lithium_Metal_Batteries/5253370][54: "Single-Ion Conducting Polymer Electrolyte with Excellent..." (HAL preprint) — https://hal.science/hal-05345120v1/file/I1000pso3cr-rev-hal.pdf][55: "Synthesis and molecular dynamic simulation of a novel single ion conducting gel polymer electrolyte for lithium-ion batteries," Polymer (2020) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0032386120303992]. Polymer–ceramic composites aim for room-temperature operation with compliance (e.g., PEO–LLZO–succinonitrile at 1.19×10⁻⁴ S/cm RT with a claimed 5.5 V window [56: "All-Solid-State Lithium Battery Fitted with Polymer Electrolyte Enhanced by Solid Plasticizer and Conductive Ceramic Filler," J. Electrochem. Soc. 165, A3558–A3565 (2018) — https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1149/2.1371814jes]) but their conduction mechanism remains under investigation [57: "Lithium-Ion Conduction Pathways in LLZO-PEO Composite Electrolytes," ACS Appl. Energy Mater. (2024) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsaem.4c02489][58: "New Insights into the Compositional Dependence of Li-Ion Transport in Polymer–Ceramic Composite Electrolytes," ACS Appl. Mater. Interfaces 10 (2018) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/acsami.7b17301][59: "Active Filler Composite Polymer Electrolytes for Lithium-Ion Batteries" (review), PMC11396385 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11396385/]. **LiPON** thin-film electrolyte (2.3±0.7×10⁻⁶ S/cm at 25 °C, 5.5 V window) is the microbattery standard; its conductivity is ~3 orders below bulk-type electrolytes, so it is only practical as ~1 µm sputtered films [60: Bates, J. B. et al., "A Stable Thin-Film Lithium Electrolyte: Lithium Phosphorus Oxynitride," J. Electrochem. Soc. 144, 524 (1997) — https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1149/1.1837443][61: "Characterization of lithium phosphorous oxynitride thin films," OSTI 211463 — https://www.osti.gov/biblio/211463][62: "All-solid-state thin-film batteries based on lithium phosphorus oxynitride" (review), IOP 2752-5724/ac7db2 — https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2752-5724/ac7db2].

## 2. Performance benchmarks vs. liquid Li-ion

### 2.1 Conductivity

- Liquid baseline: ~1 M LiPF6 in carbonates is ~**10 mS/cm class (10⁻² S/cm)** [66: Chem. Eng. J. 2024 dry-film paper — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1385894724017078].
- Sulfides: LGPS 12 mS/cm [1]; Li9.54Si1.74P1.44S11.7Cl0.3 25 mS/cm [2]; 32 mS/cm figure unverified (review-sourced) [4].
- Halides: Li3InCl6 ~1.5–2 mS/cm [39][37]; mixed-anion Li3MX6 up to **11 mS/cm** (2024) [40][41].
- Oxides: LLZO up to ~2 mS/cm best-case [67: PCCP 2018 — https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2018/cp/c7cp06768b]; LATP ~10⁻³ S/cm bulk [68: Materials 2021, PMC8510155 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8510155/][69: J. Alloys Compd. 2024 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925838824009332].
- Polymers: ~10⁻⁴ S/cm RT best cases [70: JES 2024 — https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1149/1945-7111/ad510f]; PEO needs 60–80 °C for ~10⁻³ S/cm [50].
- A 2024 claim of 0.15–0.45 S/cm for doped Li3InCl6 (Discover Chemical Engineering, Bashir & Liu) would exceed liquid electrolytes by 15–45× — far above any independently corroborated value — and is flagged as implausible and **not propagated as fact** [71: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43938-024-00055-8].

### 2.2 Cell-level metrics

- **Critical current density (CCD):** N-doped Li6PS5Cl achieved 1.52 mA/cm² at RT with 1000 h stability at 0.5 mA/cm² [72: Liu, Y. et al., JMCA 2021 — https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2021/ta/d1ta03343c]; argyrodite CCDs ">1 mA/cm²" appear only at elevated temperature/pressure [73: Tron et al., JES 170 (2023) — https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1149/1945-7111/ad01e3]; garnet plating is typically ~1 mA/cm², with 9 mA/cm² demonstrated under engineered interfaces [74: Nat. Energy 2025 — https://preview-www.nature.com/articles/s41560-025-01847-0]; an EV-relevant bar of >3 mA/cm² "not yet achieved" for LLZO [75: Krauskopf, T. et al., The natural critical current density limit for Li7La3Zr2O12 garnets, J. Mater. Chem. A 8, 15782–15788 (2020) — https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2020/ta/c9ta14177d]; at 0.09 MPa stack pressure, CCD collapses to ~50 µA/cm² [76: Critical Current Density Limitation of LLZO Solid Electrolyte, J. Electrochem. Soc. 169, 010542 (2022) — https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1149/1945-7111/ac44be]. **CCD measurement is not standardized**, so cross-study comparisons are unreliable [73][77: J. Power Sources 624, 235605 (2024) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S037877532401557X][78: Janek, J. et al., Standardizing critical current density measurements in lithium garnets, Communications Chemistry 6, 217 (2023) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s42004-023-01002-4]. A Meng-group survey found most published Li-ASSB CCDs "significantly lower than conventional lithium-ion batteries" [79: SSRN 4213169 (Meng group) — https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4213169].
- **Areal capacity:** single-cell-level assessments model ~7 mAh/cm² configurations [80: ACS Energy Lett. 2024 — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/full/10.1021/acsenergylett.4c03331]; commercial viability is argued to require >3 mAh/cm² and >3 mA/cm² [81: ChemRxiv 2021 — https://doi.org/10.33774/chemrxiv-2021-q0vbw].
- **Cycle life:** a PEO-based NMC811/Li all-solid cell showed 1000 cycles at 80% retention (45 °C) [82: Nano-Micro Lett. 16 (2024) — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40820-024-01415-3]; DOE targets call for >1000 cycles and >10-year calendar life [83: PNNL Battery500 — https://www.pnnl.gov/innovation-center-battery500-consortium][84: DOE VTO 2023 AMR Battery500 deck — https://www1.eere.energy.gov/vehiclesandfuels/downloads/2023_AMR/bat317_liu_2023_o%20-%20jun%20liu.pdf][85: OSTI 2329523 — https://www.osti.gov/biblio/2329523]. Exact >1000-cycle sulfide full-cell numbers were not verified this round (PDF-level extraction needed) [benchmarks-gaps].
- **Reproducibility reality check:** the same NMC622/Li6PS5Cl/In cell built in 21 labs delivered 106–142 mAh/g at 0.1C — a wide interlab spread [86: Puls et al., Nat. Energy 9, 1310–1320 (2024) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41560-024-01634-3].

### 2.3 Energy density

- Commercial Li-ion: 260–300 Wh/kg class, with 350–500 Wh/kg "under development" [87: OSTI 2418050 — https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/2418050]; current LIBs described as ≤650 Wh/L and ~250 Wh/kg [88: Nano-Micro Lett. 16 (2024) — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40820-024-01385-6].
- Demonstrated solid-state (lab/stack): Fraunhofer roll-to-roll sulfide pouch at **673 Wh/L / 247 Wh/kg stack** with 55 µm membranes [89: Rosner et al., Adv. Energy Mater. 15 (2025) — https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/aenm.202404790]; Si-anode sulfide cell **>400 Wh/kg** ("approaching the theoretical limit for silicon-based SSBs") [90: OSTI 2575388 — https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/2575388]; all-solid-state Li–S cell **>500 Wh/kg cell-level** claim (2025) [91: Commun. Mater. 6 (2025) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s43246-025-00918-9][92: Lin et al., Small 21, 2409536 (2025) — https://doi.org/10.1002/smll.202409536].
- Theoretical headroom: Li-metal SSBs >500 Wh/kg and >1000 Wh/L — provided thin-Li and low-excess designs work; current inhomogeneous plating forces excess Li, eroding the advantage [93: Nat. Energy 2024 — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41560-024-01676-7].
- **Skeptical counterpoint:** modeling a practical LLZO-based pouch cell yields only ~272 Wh/kg (~823 Wh/L) — barely above today's Li-ion — because of LLZO density, thick electrolyte, and packaging [94: Cheng, E. J. et al., Li-stuffed garnet solid electrolytes: current status, challenges, and perspectives, Energy Storage Materials 72, 103970 (2024); Tohoku WPI-AIMR press release (2025-03-19) — https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ensm.2024.103970].

*Assessment (inference): lab solid-state cells at ~250–400 Wh/kg sit near or modestly above today's best commercial Li-ion, but the pack-level edge is not yet demonstrated and the incumbent keeps improving (2025 record-low pack price of $108/kWh [95: BloombergNEF, Dec 9, 2025 — https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-transport/lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-fall-to-108-per-kilowatt-hour-despite-rising-metal-prices-bloombergnef/]).*

### 2.4 Anode choice

- **Li metal** offers the largest theoretical headroom (+~60–100% over Li-ion at the 500 Wh/kg class) but requires zero/low-excess designs that currently suffer coulombic-efficiency penalties [93].
- **Silicon anodes** are the lower-risk near-term compromise: >400 Wh/kg demonstrated, DOE projects targeting >350 Wh/kg and >750 Wh/L [90][85].
- **Graphite-based solid-state cells** gain little on energy density and are pursued mainly for safety/processing (inference from the above).
- **Anode-free (zero-excess)** maximizes density and simplifies manufacturing but shows accelerated short-circuiting from local Li depletion [96: Lewis, J. A. et al., Accelerated Short Circuiting in Anode-Free Solid-State Batteries Driven by Local Lithium Depletion, Adv. Energy Mater. 13, 2204186 (2023) — https://www.osti.gov/biblio/2223104] and irreversible losses [97: Anode-Less (Anode-Free) Batteries: From Fundamental Principles to Practical Pathways Toward Solid-State Implementation, Materials 19, 1232 (2026) — https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/19/6/1232].

## 3. Technical challenges and failure modes

### 3.1 Solid–solid interfacial contact and voids

Void formation during stripping is a first-order failure mode: when stripping outruns Li replenishment, voids accumulate at the Li|SE interface and later seed dendrites on plating (the "critical stripping current" effect) [98: Kasemchainan, J. et al., Critical stripping current leads to dendrite formation on plating in lithium anode solid electrolyte cells, Nat. Mater. 18, 1105–1111 (2019) — https://europepmc.org/article/MED/31358941][99: Formation, Growth, and Shrinkage of Voids in Lithium Metal in Contact With an LLZO Electrolyte (operando SEM), PMC12532089 (2025) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12532089/]. Voids reduce contact area, focus current, and raise interfacial resistance [100: Lewis, J. A. et al., Linking void and interphase evolution to electrochemistry in solid-state batteries using operando X-ray tomography, Nat. Mater. 20, 503–510 (2021) — https://doi.org/10.1038/s41563-020-00903-2]. Stack pressure is the standard mitigation (2–13 MPa in LLZO symmetric cells [101: Chang, W. et al., Evolving contact mechanics and microstructure formation dynamics of the lithium metal–Li7La3Zr2O12 interface, Nat. Commun. 12, 6369 (2021) — https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-26632-x]) but adds pack complexity; QuantumScape reports zero-external-pressure cycling in its anode-free cells and a free-standing thin-film LiPON cell cycles pressure-free — both special architectures [102: QuantumScape, SEC EX-99.1 (Q3 2021 shareholder letter, Dec 2021) — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1811414/000119312521157656/d400954dex991.htm][103: QuantumScape, Q3 2023 Letter to Shareholders (2023) — https://www.quantumscape.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/QS-Shareholder-Letter-Q3-2023.pdf][104: Cheng, D. et al., A free-standing lithium phosphorus oxynitride thin film electrolyte promotes uniformly dense lithium metal deposition with no external pressure, Nat. Nanotechnol. 18, 1448–1455 (2023) — https://doi.org/10.1038/s41565-023-01478-0]. Composite cathodes add a transport bottleneck: energy density demands SE volume fractions ≤30%, where tortuosity and poor contacts limit power [105: Mitigating the Ion Transport Tortuosity in Composite Cathodes of All-Solid-State Batteries by Wet Milling of the Solid Electrolyte Particles, ACS Appl. Energy Mater. 6 (2023) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/full/10.1021/acsaem.3c01242][106: Janek, J. & Zeier, W. G., Challenges in speeding up solid-state battery development, Nat. Energy 8, 230–240 (2023) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41560-023-01208-9].

### 3.2 Lithium dendrite penetration

The "dendrite-free" claim for ceramics is falsified: dendrites form even in dense/single-crystal-quality ceramics via local electronic conductivity at grain boundaries [107: Han, F. et al., High electronic conductivity as the origin of lithium dendrite formation within solid electrolytes, Nat. Energy 4, 187–196 (2019) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41560-018-0312-z][108: Hatzell, K. B. et al., Challenges in Lithium Metal Anodes for Solid-State Batteries, ACS Energy Lett. 5, 922–934 (2020) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsenergylett.9b02668]. The mechanism debate (grain-boundary path vs. pore initiation) was substantially reconciled by a 2023 Nature study: **initiation** occurs when Li plates into subsurface pores connected to the surface by microcracks; **propagation** is wedge-opening, with Li filling the dry crack from the rear. Notably, **lower stack pressure suppresses propagation** — the opposite of the contact-maintenance picture — creating a "stack pressure dilemma" [109: Ning, Z. et al., Dendrite initiation and propagation in lithium metal solid-state batteries, Nature 618, 287–293 (2023) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-05970-4][110: External-pressure–electrochemistry coupling in solid-state lithium metal batteries, Nature Reviews Materials 9, 305–326 (2024) — https://preview-www.nature.com/articles/s41578-024-00669-y].

### 3.3 Electrochemical stability and coatings

Sulfides are unstable against both Li metal (reduction) and high-voltage cathodes (oxidation) [65][11]; halides are oxidation-stable but reduction-unstable vs Li [44][45]; oxides are kinetically tolerant but not inert [21][26]. Cathode coatings (LiNbO3-type) are the standard fix: demonstrated for NCM622|Li6PS5Cl at 4.3 V [111: Wullich, F. et al., Optimized LiNbO3 sol-gel coatings for NCM622 in sulfide-based all-solid-state batteries (PSI, 2025) — https://www.dora.lib4ri.ch/psi/dload/psi:76172/PDF/Wullich-2025-Optimized_LiNbO3_sol-gel_coatings_for-(published_version).pdf], but coating quality/route matters and thick coatings introduce their own problems at ≥4.4 V [112: Revealing and Overcoming Unfavorable Electrochemical Behaviors of Thick LiNbO3-Coated NCM523 for All-Solid-State Lithium Batteries, J. Electrochem. Soc. 171, 040529 (2024) — https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1149/2754-2734/ad4b40].

### 3.4 Moisture sensitivity and dry rooms

Sulfides evolve toxic H2S on water exposure [7][8][9] and are described as "incompatible with today's lithium-ion battery manufacturing infrastructure" [113: Surface molecular engineering to enable processing of sulfide solid electrolytes in humid ambient air, Nat. Commun. 15, 10560 (2024) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-55634-8]. Sulfide processing reportedly needs <−45 °C dew point vs ~−32 °C for Li-ion, with 2–5× dehumidification demand (single trade-press source) [114: The Elec, CK Solution Develops Dehumidifier for Solid-State Battery Production (2025) — https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=11136]. Oxides are more tolerant but LLZO still degrades in air (CO2/H2O → Li2CO3) [25]; halides sit in between [47][48].

### 3.5 Manufacturing and economics

- LLZO requires sintering >1200 °C [19][20]; early-scale LLZ material price ~US$2000/kg (single source; model/preprint) [115: Schnell, J. et al., Prospects on production technologies and manufacturing cost of oxide-based all-solid-state lithium batteries (Forschungszentrum Jülich, 2020) — https://juser.fz-juelich.de/record/872556/files/20200120%20Prospects%20on%20production%20technologies%20and%20manufacturing%20cost%20for%20oxide%20based%20all-solid-state%20lithium%20batteries.pdf].
- Li-metal anode must cost ≈US$2.08/m² to match graphite cost in modeled cells [93].
- Incumbent economics: Li-ion pack $108/kWh (2025), with 2035 projections of $75–90/kWh (NMC) and $55–65/kWh (LFP) [116: McKinsey Center for Future Mobility, Battery 2035: Building new advantages (2025) — https://www.mckinsey.com/features/mckinsey-center-for-future-mobility/our-insights/battery-2035-building-new-advantages][95]. DOE blueprint targets <$60/kWh at-scale solid-state by 2030 (single secondary source) [117: Motavalli, J., Can Solid-State Batteries Commercialize by 2030?, Tech Briefs/SAE Media (2025) — https://www.techbriefs.com/component/content/article/50354-can-solid-state-batteries-commercialize-by-2030]; USABC targets $125/kWh [93].
- Solid-state cost estimates span ~2.3× Li-ion with "no parity this decade" (CRU) [118: CRU Group — "Unlocking a new era with all-solid-state batteries" (2025) — https://www.crugroup.com/en/communities/thought-leadership/2025/from-smartphones-to-flying-taxis-the-coming-age-of-all-solid-state-batteries/] to self-reported BYD $70/kWh parity goal [119: Intelligent Living — "BYD Solid-State Battery Production to Begin in 2027" (Aug 2026) — https://www.intelligentliving.co/byd-solid-state-battery-production-2027/].
- **Recycling is an open gap** — SSB-specific recovery is immature and "recyclable" claims unproven [120: Recycling of solid-state batteries (Review), Nature Energy (2024) — https://preview-www.nature.com/articles/s41560-024-01463-4][121: Albertus, P. et al., Challenges for and Pathways toward Li-Metal-Based All-Solid-State Batteries, ACS Energy Lett. 6, 1399–1408 (2021) — https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1775448].
- **Safety is not automatic:** solid electrolytes remove flammable liquid, but Li-metal SSBs can still release substantial heat in failure [122: Bates, A. M. et al., Are solid-state batteries safer than lithium-ion batteries?, Joule 6, 742–755 (2022) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2542435122000885].

## 5. Where the field disagrees

1. **Which chemistry wins at scale** (sulfide processability vs. oxide stability vs. halide balance vs. polymer simplicity) — explicitly unresolved in the major reviews [106][110][94].
2. **Does solid-state deliver the energy-density promise?** The >500 Wh/kg/>1000 Wh/L targets [93][121] clash with cell-level models yielding ~272 Wh/kg for practical LLZO pouch cells [94] and with benchmarking showing how far lab cells lag [221: Randau, S. et al., Benchmarking the performance of all-solid-state lithium batteries, Nat. Energy 5, 259–270 (2020) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41560-020-0565-1].
3. **Dendrite mechanisms** (grain-boundary electronic conductivity vs. pore/crack initiation) — largely reconciled by the 2023 Nature initiation/propagation model, but grain-boundary-softening and pore-initiation literature continues [109][107][222: Yu, S. & Siegel, D. J., Grain boundary softening: a potential mechanism for lithium metal penetration through stiff solid electrolytes, ACS Appl. Mater. Interfaces 10, 38151–38158 (2018) — https://doi.org/10.1021/acsami.8b17223].
4. **Stack pressure**: required for most inorganic systems [223: Doux, J.-M. et al., Pressure effects on sulfide electrolytes for all solid-state batteries, J. Mater. Chem. A 8, 5049–5055 (2020) — https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2020/ta/c9ta12889a][224: The Critical Stack Pressure to Alter Void Generation at Li/Solid-Electrolyte Interfaces during Stripping, J. Electrochem. Soc. 169, 100521 (2022) — https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1149/1945-7111/ac91aa] vs. zero-pressure architectures (QuantumScape, thin-film LiPON) [102][104]; and lower pressure *helps* against propagation while higher pressure *helps* contact — the "stack pressure dilemma" [109][110].
5. **Semi-solid as bridge or destination** — Chinese experts publicly disagree; 2026 is called "mass production year" for semi-solid while all-solid "still gains momentum" [225: Shanghai Metals Market, Experts Debate: Solid-Liquid Hybrid Battery Mass Production Year Begins, All-Solid-State Still Gaining Momentum (2025) — https://news.metal.com/newscontent/104018741-experts-debate-solid-liquid-hybrid-battery-mass-production-year-begins-all-solid-state-still-gaining-momentum-smm-analys].
6. **Cost-parity timing** — from BYD's claimed $70/kWh goal [119] to CRU's "not this decade" [118].
7. **CCD and "dendrite-free" claims** — unstandardized protocols make headline numbers unreliable; several high-profile claims have been contested [78][108][73].

## 6. Open questions

1. Can thin (≤50 µm), defect-free ceramic/halide membranes be manufactured at scale — bridging the gap between 500 µm lab press pellets and commercial pouch cells? [86][94]
2. Can interfaces be engineered to remove the stack-pressure requirement (creep, lithiophilic interlayers, thin Li)? [98][99][224][93]
3. Can sulfides be processed outside dry rooms (surface passivation, humid-air processing) or dry-room cost lowered? [113][114]
4. Does the >500 Wh/kg promise survive realistic pack modeling, especially for oxide and polymer routes? [94][221][93]
5. Will anode-free cells reach commercial coulombic efficiency and short-circuit resistance? [96][93][226: Electro-chemo-mechanics of anode-free solid-state batteries (Perspective), Nature Materials (2025) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41563-024-02055-z]
6. What does recycling of solid-state cells look like — and are "recyclable" claims real? [120][121]
7. When does the first *full*-solid-state automotive battery actually ship, and does the market wait for it or settle for semi-solid? [126][184: Green Car Reports — "Solid-state batteries aren't likely for Hyundai or Kia before 2030" (Feb 2025) — https://www.greencarreports.com/news/1145890_hyundai-kia-solid-state-batteries-before-2030-not-likely][211][225]
