# Researcher Brief T4 — Challenges, Failure Modes & Open Problems

**Project:** Deep research on solid-state battery electrolytes.
**Your output file:** `outputs/.drafts/solid-state-battery-electrolytes-research-challenges.md`

## Scope

Document the dominant technical and economic challenges that keep solid-state electrolytes from mass adoption, and the field's open problems. Complement T1–T3; do not duplicate their content (e.g., chemistry basics or company news), except where a challenge is chemistry-specific.

## Questions to answer

1. Solid–solid interfacial contact: void formation, contact loss on cycling, interfacial resistance growth, cathode–electrolyte composite design. Sourced evidence and reviews.
2. Lithium dendrite penetration through ceramic electrolytes: short-circuit mechanisms, critical current density limits, whether "dendrite-free" claims hold. Note the debate about whether dendrites grow through grain boundaries or pores.
3. Electrochemical stability windows: reduction/oxidation of sulfide vs. oxide electrolytes vs. Li metal and high-voltage cathodes; coating strategies (e.g., LiNbO3-type coatings).
4. Moisture/air sensitivity: H2S generation from sulfides, dry-room requirements and cost; halide vs. oxide tolerance.
5. Mechanical properties: ductility of sulfides vs. brittle oxides, stack pressure requirements (some cells need high external pressure — report typical MPa values with sources).
6. Manufacturing/scale-up economics: sintering temperatures (LLZO ~1000+ °C), cost of Li metal anode, cell assembly, recycling, and material cost vs. lithium-ion incumbent.
7. Open problems and where experts disagree: e.g., is all-solid-state needed vs. semi-solid? Does solid-state actually deliver the promised energy density at pack level? Anode-free viability.
