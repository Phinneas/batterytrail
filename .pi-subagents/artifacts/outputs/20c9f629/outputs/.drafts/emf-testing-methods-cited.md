## Executive Summary

"EMF testing" is three distinct families of procedure, each with its own standards stack:

1. **EMC emissions & immunity testing** — verifying that electronic products do not emit excessive electromagnetic interference (CISPR 16 receiver methods, FCC Part 15/CISPR 32 limits) [1, 5, 7, 13] and that they tolerate external fields (IEC 61000-4-x immunity tests) [13, 15, 17].
2. **Human RF exposure / SAR testing** — measuring how much RF energy a wireless device deposits in a simulated human (phantom-based SAR per IEC/IEEE 62209-1528, FCC KDB procedures [23, 27, 28, 29]; power-density measurement above 6 GHz per IEC/IEEE 63195-1 for 5G mmWave [41, 42, 43]).
3. **Environmental EMF surveying** — measuring fields near base stations, power lines, EVs, and in workplaces/homes against ICNIRP/FCC exposure limits [34, 70] (broadband vs frequency-selective instruments [49, 50, 51]; spot vs long-term monitoring [66, 67]; ICNIRP 2020 averaging conventions [39, 70]).

Key findings:

- **Procedure is standardized end-to-end but split by jurisdiction.** The same device faces different limits and averaging: FCC 1.6 W/kg over 1 g tissue vs ICNIRP/CE 2 W/kg over 10 g [71, 72, 77]; FCC 30-min general-public averaging vs ICNIRP 2020 30-min whole-body / 6-min local averaging [39, 70]; above 6 GHz, ICNIRP 2020 switched to absorbed power density over 4 cm² (plus 1 cm² >30 GHz) [34, 39], while FCC kept plane-wave-equivalent MPE (1 mW/cm² public above 1.5 GHz) [70].
- **SAR testing is the costliest routine procedure:** ~$3,000–$15,000+ per device, 3 days–3 weeks [27, 106], with phantom + robotic probe systems (SPEAG DASY class) [30, 31], tissue-equivalent liquids tuned per frequency [30], area+zoom scans [35, 36, 37, 38], and expanded uncertainty budgets typically ~±22–26% (k=2) [31, 37]. mmWave power-density measurement carries even larger uncertainty (~±29%) [48].
- **Environmental surveys have national protocols with concrete procedural details** — e.g., France's ANFR protocol (1.5 m sweep, average of 1.1/1.5/1.7 m heights, 6-minute RMS, broadband-then-selective workflow) [57, 58], Switzerland's SwissNIS monitoring (median exposure <1% of limits) [128], while Canada's ISED uses 0.2–1.8 m spatial averaging [59]. No globally uniform survey protocol exists [59, 60, 61].
- **Measured exposures are far below limits in most environments:** Swiss national monitoring (medians <1% of the 50 V/m limit) [128], French 5G campaigns (>13,000 in-situ measurements, all below limits) [126, 129], EV in-cabin fields (~20% of ICNIRP at feet, ~2% at head) [113], EV wireless charging within ICNIRP 2010 at rated operation [124].
- **Where procedures are weakest:** 5G maximum-exposure extrapolation has no standardized factor [127]; uplink and body-worn (wearable) exposure is under-measured [146, 147, 148]; consumer-grade EMF meters are largely unbenchmarked against research instruments (an EPRI evaluation was at project stage) [149, 150, 151, 152]; EMF hypersensitivity is real but has no validated test procedure and provocation studies are negative [142, 143, 144]; ICNIRP 2010's alleged "24 h averaging for general public" could not be verified from primary text [78] [standards-gaps Q1].

## 1. The three families of EMF testing

### 1.1 EMC emissions and immunity (product compliance)

**Emissions procedures.** Conducted emissions are measured at the mains port via a LISN (Line Impedance Stabilization Network; the 50 Ω/50 µH V-AMN is standard for 150 kHz–30 MHz, CISPR 16-1-2 [2: IEC, "CISPR 16-1-2:2014" (iTeh sample PDF), 2014 — https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/18695/f3ea20c6b2d642878c420c3c571a2ea3/CISPR-16-1-2-2014.pdf]) using an EMI receiver per CISPR 16-1-1 with 9 kHz resolution bandwidth and quasi-peak + average detectors [1: In Compliance Magazine, "EMI Measurement Receiver Requirements (CISPR 16-1-1)" (n.d.) — https://incompliancemag.com/emi-measurement-receiver-requirements-cispr-16-1-1/; 3: IEC, "CISPR 16-2-1:2014+AMD1:2017" (iTeh sample PDF), 2014 — https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/18696/f3c3746fb82c476aad484aa1cd14232f/CISPR-16-2-1-2014.pdf; 4: Spilma, "Conducted emissions: LISN method and CISPR + FCC limits" (n.d.) — https://www.spilma.com/en/guides/conducted-emissions-lisn-test]. Radiated emissions use an antenna + receiver on a validated site — semi-anechoic chamber (SAC) below 1 GHz, fully anechoic room (FAR) above — with OATS as the reference concept; site validation via normalized site attenuation (±4 dB) below 1 GHz and site VSWR (6 dB criterion) above [5: Spilma, "Radiated emissions EMC test: pre-scan and final scan" (n.d.) — https://www.spilma.com/en/guides/radiated-emissions-emc-test; 6: Spilma, "EMC chamber types: SAC, FAR, OATS, GTEM, reverberation" (n.d.) — https://www.spilma.com/en/guides/emc-chamber-types-sac-far-oats-reverb]. CISPR receiver bandwidths: 200 Hz (9–150 kHz), 9 kHz (150 kHz–30 MHz), 120 kHz (30 MHz–1 GHz), 1 MHz (1–18 GHz); quasi-peak time constants are specified per band [1]. Practical strategy: fast peak pre-scan, then quasi-peak final scan only on candidates within ~6 dB of the limit [5]. Typical limits: FCC Part 15 Class B radiated 40/43.5/46/54 dBµV/m at 3 m (30–88/88–216/216–960/>960 MHz) [7: LearnEMC, "Introduction to EMC Regulations and Standards" (n.d.) — https://learnemc.com/emc-regulations-and-standards]; CISPR 32 Class B 40 dBµV/m at 3 m (30–230 MHz) [8: rftools.io, "Radiated Emission Estimate calculator" (n.d.) — https://rftools.io/calculators/emc/radiated-emission-estimate/; 9: AESTECHNO, "EMC margin to CISPR/FCC limit calculator" (n.d.) — https://www.aestechno.com/en/tools/emc-margin/]. Alternative sites: GTEM cells (pre-compliance; three-orientation total radiated power) and reverberation chambers (IEC 61000-4-21; statistically uniform fields, 10–20 dB less amplifier power) [6, 10: IEEE ISEMC 2000, "Total-radiated-power-based OATS-equivalent emissions testing in reverberation chambers and GTEM cells," 2000 — https://doi.org/10.1109/isemc.2000.875531; 11: ERETEC, "GTEM/TEM Cells & Reverberation Chambers" (n.d.) — https://www.eretec.com/en/product/gtem-tem-cells-striplines-reverberation-chambers/; 12: EMC-directory, "What is an Electromagnetic Reverberation Chamber?" (n.d.) — https://www.emc-directory.com/community/what-is-an-electromagnetic-reverberation-chamber].

**Immunity procedures.** IEC 61000-4-3 (radiated RF immunity, ed. 4:2020, 80 MHz–6 GHz): 80% AM at 1 kHz, severity levels 3 V/m (residential) and 10 V/m (industrial), uniform field area (1.5 × 1.5 m, 4×4 grid, −0/+6 dB, ≥12/16 points), sweep step ≤1%, dwell ≥2× EUT time constant, pass/fail via performance criteria A/B/C [13: IEC, "IEC 61000-4-3:2020" (webstore), 2020 — https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/59849; 14: Spilma, "IEC 61000-4-3: radiated RF field immunity" (n.d.) — https://www.spilma.com/en/guides/iec-61000-4-3-radiated-rf-immunity]. IEC 61000-4-6 (conducted immunity, 150 kHz–80 MHz per the 2023 edition; some vendors cite 9 kHz–80 MHz — flagged discrepancy) uses CDNs/EM clamps at 1/3/10 V levels [15: IEC, "IEC 61000-4-6:2023" (webstore), 2023 — https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/65586; 16: ATECORP, "IEC 61000-4-6 Testing" (n.d.) — https://www.atecorp.com/compliance-standards/iec/iec-61000-4-6]. IEC 61000-4-8 (power-frequency magnetic field): preferred continuous levels 1–100 A/m (1 A/m ≈ 1.26 µT) plus short-duration 300/1000 A/m [17: IEC, "IEC 61000-4-8:2009" (webstore), 2009 — https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/4229; 18: IEC, "IEC 61000-4-8:2009 RLV" (iTeh sample PDF), 2009 — https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/16569/ee75262796e14628a75ed213c2f26cce/IEC-61000-4-8-2009.pdf; 19: CElectronics, "Power Frequency Magnetic Field Immunity Testing" (n.d.) — https://www.celectronics.com/EMC-EMI-Testing/Power-Frequency-Magnetic-Field-Testing]; IEC 61000-4-9 (pulse magnetic field): 6.4/16 µs impulse, 100/300/1000 A/m [20: iTeh, "IEC 61000-4-9:2016," 2016 — https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/iec/ae5c061a-58ba-4b39-83e3-0daa237b6776/iec-61000-4-9-2016; 21: ATECORP, "IEC 61000-4-9 Testing" (n.d.) — https://www.atecorp.com/compliance-standards/iec/iec-61000-4-9; 22: Absolute EMC, "IEC/EN 61000-4-9" (n.d.) — https://absolute-emc.com/standard/International_Test_Standards/iecen-61000-4-9].

### 1.2 Human RF exposure / SAR measurement

**Standards.** IEC/IEEE 62209-1528:2020 (4 MHz–10 GHz) is the consolidated protocol for conservative peak spatial-average SAR in a simplified head/body model, replacing IEC 62209-1:2016 (next-to-ear, 300 MHz–6 GHz), IEC 62209-2:2010 (body proximity, 30 MHz–6 GHz) and IEEE 1528-2013 (head) [23: IEC, "IEC/IEEE 62209-1528:2020" (webstore), 2020 — https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/62753; 24: IEC, "IEC 62209-1:2016" (webstore), 2016 — https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/25336; 25: IEC, "IEC 62209-2:2010+A1:2019 CSV" (webstore), 2019 — https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/65156; 26: IEEE SA, "IEEE/IEC 62209-1528-2020," 2020 — https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/62209-1528/7325/]. FCC procedure is KDB-driven: 447498 (general RF exposure guidance; portable ≤20 cm vs mobile >20 cm; exemption thresholds — the Bluetooth exemption at ≤5 mm dropped from 10 mW to 3 mW, pulling BLE wearables into testing) and 865664 (SAR measurement requirements 100 MHz–6 GHz) [27: MarkReady, "SAR Testing Requirements" (n.d.) — https://markready.io/learn/sar-testing-requirements; 28: FCC OET, "KDB 447498 D01 v06 General RF Exposure Guidance" (n.d.) — https://apps.fcc.gov/kdb/GetAttachment.html?desc=447498+D01+General+RF+Exposure+Guidance+v06&id=f8IQgJxTTL5y0oRi0cpAuA%3D%3D; 29: FCC OET, "KDB 865664 D01 SAR Measurement 100 MHz to 6 GHz v01r04," 2015 — https://apps.fcc.gov/kdb/GetAttachment.html?desc=865664+D01+SAR+Measurement+100+MHz+to+6+GHz+v01r04&id=RUMcMDL7fmDLsdRSsbCNoA%3D%3D&tracking_number=28242].

**Hardware and procedure.** SAM phantom (Specific Anthropomorphic Mannequin, adult-male 90th-percentile head geometry) or flat phantom for body, filled with tissue-equivalent liquid whose permittivity/conductivity are tuned to the test frequency (a 900 MHz mix is not valid at 5 GHz) [30: Spilma, "SAR procedures: absorption rate (IEC 62209, EN 50360)" (n.d.) — https://www.spilma.com/en/guides/sar-procedures-iec-62209-en-50360]; miniature three-axis dosimetric E-field probes on a six-axis robot (SPEAG DASY is the de facto platform; positioning <0.2 mm) [31: SPEAG, "DASY8 Module SAR / APD" (n.d.) — https://speag.swiss/products/dasy8/m-sar-apd]; liquid dielectric validation at the start of each day and temperature control [30, 32: IOP Measurement Science and Technology, "Precise dielectric property measurements and E-field probe calibration for SAR measurements using a rectangular waveguide," 2009 — https://doi.org/10.1088/0957-0233/20/4/045702; 33: PMC, same waveguide-calibration paper (PMC5839153), 2009 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5839153/; 34: ICNIRP, "Guidelines for Limiting Exposure to Electromagnetic Fields (100 kHz to 300 GHz)," Health Physics 118(5), 2020 — https://www.icnirp.org/cms/upload/publications/ICNIRPrfgdl2020.pdf]. Measurement flow: power reference → coarse area scan to locate the maximum → fine zoom scan (e.g., 7×7×7 points over 30×30×30 mm at 5 mm) → interpolation/extrapolation to the phantom surface → integration over a 1 g (FCC) or 10 g (ICNIRP/CE) cube → drift check [35: PMC, "Discrepancies of Measured SAR between Traditional and Fast Scanning Systems" (PMC7143529), 2020 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7143529/; 36: SPEAG, "Faster Determination of Mass-Averaged SAR From 2-D Scans" (n.d.) — https://speag.swiss/assets/downloads/products/isar/free_downloads/Mass-average-SAR-from-2D-scans-MTT-version.pdf; 37: UL SAR evaluation report, FCC ID 2AU47-00001 (Boxine Toniebox), via fcc.report, 2020 — https://fcc.report/FCC-ID/2AU47-00001/4874603.pdf; 38: fcc.report, "Zoom Scan Uncertainty" (CRN 25568), FCC ID PPD-AR5BCB-00032 (n.d.) — https://fcc.report/FCC-ID/PPD-AR5BCB-00032/348145.pdf]. Positioning: cheek/touch and tilt positions for the head, body positions at manufacturer-declared separation; worst-case channels and simultaneous-transmission combinations — typically >100 configurations for a multi-band phone [30]. Typical expanded uncertainty (k=2): ~±26% for 1 g body SAR <3 GHz in a KDB-865664-style report [37]; the numerical "maximum expanded uncertainty" cap in IEC 62209-1 §7.3 could not be verified (PDF blocked) [24] [methods-gaps].

**Above 6 GHz (5G FR2).** SAR averaging over 1/10 g loses physical meaning as penetration depth falls (~1 cm at 6 GHz to ~0.4 mm at 300 GHz) [34]; ICNIRP 2020 switches to absorbed power density with a 4 cm² averaging area (plus 1 cm² >30 GHz, brief-exposure restrictions) [34, 39: ICNIRP, "Differences between the ICNIRP (2020) and previous guidelines," 2020 — https://www.icnirp.org/en/differences.html; 40: PMC, "ICNIRP Guidelines' Exposure Assessment Method for 5G" (PMC10094038), 2023 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10094038/]. Measurement standards: IEC TR 63170:2018 (6–100 GHz) → IEC/IEEE 63195-1:2022 (power density near head/body, 6–300 GHz; 2nd edition in draft 2026) [41: IEEE SA, "IEEE/IEC 63195-1-2022," 2022 — https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/63195-1/7357/; 42: IEC, "IEC/IEEE 63195-1:2022" (webstore), 2022 — https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/62755; 43: IEC, "IEC TR 63170:2018" (webstore), 2018 — https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/62012; 44: IEC, "IEC TR 63170:2018" (iTeh sample PDF), 2018 — https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/101591/cae89bd027b4429a9ca2a99ca4cb5964/IEC-TR-63170-2018.pdf]. FCC guidance: KDB 842590 (updated March 2025), tiered evaluation vs 10 W/m² general public (6–100 GHz) [45: FCC OET, "KDB 842590: Millimeter Wave Device Measurement Procedures" (updated 2025-03-06) — https://apps.fcc.gov/oetcf/kdb/forms/FTSSearchResultPage.cfm?id=240776&switch=P; 46: RF Essentials, "MPE Requirement for 5G FR2 Device and How It Is Tested" (n.d.) — https://rfessentials.com/rf-knowledge-base/what-is-the-mpe-maximum-permissible-exposure-requirement-for-a-5g-fr2-device-and/]. Example result: a 5G FR2 flagship reported psPD 0.679 mW/cm² (n261) and 0.560 mW/cm² (n260) — far below the 10 W/m² (=1 mW/cm²) public limit [47: PCTEST near-field power density evaluation report, FCC ID A3LSMN986U (Samsung SM-N986U), via fcc.report, 2020 — https://fcc.report/FCC-ID/A3LSMN986U/4778266.pdf]. mmWave measurement uncertainty is large: SPEAG's interim 6–10 GHz procedure quotes ±29.0% (1 cm²) and ±28.8% (4 cm²) expanded uncertainty [48: SPEAG, "DASY8 Application Note: Interim Procedures for APD & PD at 6–10 GHz" (Apr 2022) — https://speag.swiss/assets/downloads/products/dasy/application-notes/Measurements-6-10-GHz/AppNote-6-10GHz-220405.pdf].

### 1.3 Environmental EMF surveying

**Broadband vs frequency-selective.** Broadband isotropic probes give a single total-field value for first-line screening; frequency-selective (spectrum analyzer + antenna) is required when results approach limits or when per-service attribution is needed (e.g., base-station operator contribution) [49: Wavecontrol, "Different EMF Measuring Methods: Broadband vs Selective" (n.d.) — https://www.wavecontrol.com/news/different-emf-measuring-methods-broadband-vs-selective/; 50: Keysight, "How to Measure EMF" (n.d.) — https://www.keysight.com/used/us/en/knowledge/guides/how-to-measure-emf; 51: Wikipedia, "EMF measurement" (n.d.) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMF_measurement; 52: Narda Safety Test Solutions, "SRM-3006" (n.d.) — https://www.narda-sts.com/en/products/emf-measuring-devices-and-solutions/srm-3006/]. ETSI EG 202 373 and IEC 62232 (base stations, updated 2025) define the assessment framework; ITU-T K.83 (monitoring) and K.91 (assessment) cover the telecom side [53: ETSI, "EG 202 373 V1.1.1 Guide to the methods of measurement of RF fields" (n.d.) — https://etsi.org/deliver/etsi_eg/202300_202399/202373/01.01.01_60/eg_202373v010101p.pdf; 54: IEC, "IEC 62232:2025" (webstore), 2025 — https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/89073; 55: ITU-T, "Recommendation K.83 (01/2024) Monitoring of electromagnetic field levels," 2024 — https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-t/rec/k/T-REC-K.83-202401-S!!TOC-HTM-E.htm; 56: ITU-T, "Recommendation K.91 (01/2024) Guidance for assessment, evaluation and monitoring of human exposure to RF EMF," 2024 — https://www.itu.int/epublications/zh/publication/itu-t-k-91-2024-01-guidance-for-assessment-evaluation-and-monitoring-of-human-exposure-to-radio-frequency-electromagnetic-fields].

**Procedural conventions vary by jurisdiction.** ANFR (France): probe swept at 1.5 m to find the maximum-exposure point, then average of measurements at 1.1/1.5/1.7 m; 6-minute RMS per service for the frequency-selective step; broadband result above 6 V/m forces detailed analysis [57: ANFR, "Déroulement d'une mesure" (n.d.) — https://www.anfr.fr/maitriser/les-installations-radioelectriques/deroulement-dune-mesure; 58: ANFR, "Protocole de mesure" v15-4.1, 2019 — https://www.anfr.fr/fileadmin/mediatheque/documents/expace/Protocole-mesure-15-4.1.pdf]. ISED (Canada) GL-01: spatial averaging between 0.2 and 1.8 m below 3 GHz, spatial maximum above; non-conductive tripod [59: ISED Canada, "GL-01 Issue 4 (2021) Guidelines for the Measurement of RF Fields at Frequencies from 3 kHz to 300 GHz," 2021 — https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/sites/default/files/attachments/2022/GL-01-i4-2021-12EN.pdf]. ECC REC(02)04: measurement points chosen as the highest expected exposure [60: CEPT ECC, "ECC Recommendation (02)04," 2002 — https://docdb.cept.org/download/1889]. ITU-R SM.2452-1 commonly assumes 1.5 m height [61: ITU-R, "Report SM.2452-1 (2022) Electromagnetic field measurements to assess human exposure," 2022 — https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-r/opb/rep/R-REP-SM.2452-1-2022-PDF-E.pdf]. Low-frequency (50/60 Hz) surveys follow IEC 61786-1/-2 (instruments and procedures, 1 Hz–100 kHz) and IEEE 644 (power-frequency fields from overhead lines) [62: IEC, "IEC 61786-1:2013+AMD1:2024 CSV" (webstore), 2024 — https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/98656; 63: IEC, "IEC 61786-2:2014" (webstore), 2014 — https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/5907; 64: IEEE SA, "IEEE 644-2019," 2019 — https://standards.ieee.org/standard/644-2019.html; 65: IEC, "IEC 61786-1:2013" (webstore), 2013 — https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/5906].

**Spot vs long-term monitoring.** Spot measurements characterize instantaneous exposure; fixed exposimeters over 24 h–weeks capture traffic dependence (a Naples study found short daily-hours measurements not always representative of the daily average) [66: PMC, "RF-EMF Exposure Assessment in Indoor Environments" (review, PMC6466609), 2019 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6466609/; 67: University of Naples, "High Noon for Mobile Networks: Short-Time EMF Measurements to Capture Daily Exposure" (n.d.) — https://iris.unina.it/retrieve/e2808d23-e776-43c3-b280-e1652af97944/High_Noon_for_Mobile_Networks_Short-Time_EMF_Measurements_to_Capture_Daily_Exposure.pdf]. Personal exposimeters add body-shielding uncertainty; distributed multi-antenna designs reduce it [68: MDPI Sensors, "A Personal, Distributed Exposimeter: Procedure for Design, Calibration, Validation, and Application," 2016 — https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/16/2/180; 69: Environment International, "Exposure to radiofrequency electromagnetic fields: Comparison of exposimeters with a novel body-worn distributed meter," 2021 — https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envint.2021.106711].

### 2.3 Compliance workflow

Applicable limits → accredited (ISO/IEC 17025; FCC-recognized) test lab → test per KDB/CISPR/IEC procedures → report per 17025 cl. 7.8 (and KDB 865664 structure for SAR: configurations, conducted power, tissue parameters, system check vs reference dipoles within ±10%, uncertainty budget) [94, 95, 102: fcc.report, "SAR report per KDB 865664, FCC ID Z64-WL18SBMOD" (n.d.) — https://fcc.report/FCC-ID/Z64-WL18SBMOD/2811574.pdf; 103: fccid.co, "SAR Report Appendix B — tissue liquids, system checks, validation" (n.d.) — https://fccid.co/api/files/s2/fccids/IPH-A5172/files/864c3a01_SAR_Report_App_B.pdf] → US: TCB review → FCC grant; EU: DoC → CE mark [104: MarkReady, "FCC Certification Cost," 2026 — https://markready.io/learn/fcc-certification-cost]. Re-test after first-pass failure is the largest hidden cost: ~50% of consumer electronics fail EMC on first attempt; pre-compliance testing drops the first-pass failure rate to <10% [104, 105: Compatible Electronics, "Common EMC Test Failures" (n.d.) — https://www.celectronics.com/Resources/Common-EMC-Failures].

## 3. Applications and lab practice

### 3.1 Consumer wireless devices (SAR)

Workflow: portable device (<20 cm from body) → SAR evaluation per KDB 447498/865664; mobile device (>20 cm) → MPE calculation only; FDTD simulation accepted only as a supplement [27]. Costs: $3,000–$5,000 single-band wearable (3–5 days); $10,000–$15,000+ multi-band phone (1–3 weeks); re-test $3,000–$8,000; SAR exemption/MPE analysis $500–$2,000 [27, 106: Compliance Testing, "How Much Does SAR Testing Cost?" (n.d.) — https://compliancetesting.com/sar-testing-cost/]. Labs: TÜV SÜD/Rheinland, UL, Element, Bureau Veritas (FR1 SAR + FR2 power density), RF Exposure Lab [107: TÜV SÜD, "SAR Test Services" (n.d.) — https://www.tuvsud.com/en/services/testing/specific-absorption-rate-sar-testing; 108: TÜV Rheinland, "Specific Absorption Rate (SAR) Testing" (n.d.) — https://www.tuv.com/world/en/specific-absorption-rate-(sar)-testing.html; 109: UL, "Specific Absorption Rate (SAR) Testing" (n.d.) — https://www.ul.com/services/specific-absorption-rate-sar-testing; 110: Element, "Specific Absorption Rate (SAR) Testing" (n.d.) — https://www.element.com/connected-technologies/wireless-testing/specific-absorption-rate-sar-testing; 111: Bureau Veritas CPS, "5G FR1 & FR2 Conformance Testing" (n.d.) — https://www.cps.bureauveritas.com/needs/5g-fr1-fr2-conformance-testing; 112: RF Exposure Lab, "Millimeter Wave Technology Testing Equipment" (n.d.) — https://rfexposurelab.com/millimeter-wave-technology-testing-equipment/]. Full FCC certification for a cellular smartphone with carrier certification runs $50,000–$200,000+ over 6–9 months [104].

### 3.2 Electric vehicles

In-cabin ELF surveys: published studies measure B at head/chest/feet during driving and charging, evaluating against ICNIRP reference levels with frequency weighting [113: SINTEF, "Measurement of the magnetic field inside electric vehicles" (n.d.) — https://www.sintef.no/projectweb/em-safety/project-results/measurement-of-the-magnetic-field-inside-electric-vehicles/; 114: IJERPH, "Long-Term Monitoring of Extremely Low Frequency Magnetic Fields in Electric Vehicles," 2019 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6801816/]. SINTEF (8 EVs + 3 ICE vehicles): max exposure at feet ≈20% of ICNIRP reference level at start-up (≈10% for combustion), ≈2% at head [113]. Two-year monitoring of shared EVs: B from ~0.17–0.19 µT (constant speed) to 0.74–1.57 µT (acceleration); values shifted after component replacement (up to 0.54 µT), prompting calls for post-repair re-measurement — not currently required by standards [114]. Procedure standard: IEC 62764-1:2022 (in-vehicle LF magnetic field measurement for passenger cars) [115: IEC, "IEC 62764-1:2022" (webstore), 2022 — https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/68086]; automotive EMC immunity (ISO 11451/11452, UNECE R10) governs vehicle EMC, not human exposure [116: EUR-Lex, "UN Regulation No. 10 (consolidated 2022)," 2022 — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:42022X2263; 117: Applus+ IDIADA, "New UN R10 EMC Regulation Series 07 Now in Force," 2025 — https://www.applusidiada.com/global/en/news/new-series-07-of-amendments-to-un-r10-regulation-on-electromagnetic-compatibility-enters-into-force; 118: ISO, "ISO 11451-1:2025," 2025 — https://www.iso.org/standard/11451-1; 119: ISO, "ISO 11452-1:2025," 2025 — https://www.iso.org/standard/83225.html].

Wireless charging (WPT): SAE J2954 (2024 edition) defines WPT1–3 levels (3.7–11.1 kVA) and alignment methodology [120: SAE, "J2954_202408," 2024 — https://saemobilus.sae.org/standards/j2954_202408-wireless-power-transfer-light-duty-plug-electric-vehicles-alignment-methodology; 121: SAE, "J2954 Wireless Power Transfer for Light-Duty Plug-in/Electric Vehicles and Alignment Methodology" (n.d.) — https://www.sae.org/standards/content/j2954]; IEC 61980 series covers system requirements [122: IEC, "IEC 61980-2:2023" (webstore), 2023 — https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/66046]; EMF exposure evaluation via IEC TR 62905 → IEC/IEEE 63184 (2025) for inductive WPT (snippet-verified) [123: arXiv:2510.18570, "EMF Exposure Assessment and ... (WPT exposure evaluation)," 2025 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.18570]. A 2025 simulation of 11.1 kVA J2954-compliant pads found all configurations within ICNIRP 2010 limits (B ≤ 15 µT, E ≤ 83 V/m at evaluation points), with some configurations needing 6–9% power reduction to meet the older 1998 limits [124: Scientific Reports, "Safety assessment of electromagnetic fields of different transmitters and receivers for EVs static charging," 2025 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12043850/].

### 3.3 5G infrastructure and environmental monitoring

Procedures: IEC 62232 (2025) for base-station field/PD/SAR determination [54]; ITU-T K Suppl. 16 (5G compliance assessment) [125: ITU-T, "Supplement K Suppl. 16 (10/2022): EMF compliance assessments for 5G wireless networks," 2022 — https://www.itu.int/epublications/publication/itu-t-k-suppl-16-2022-10-electromagnetic-field-compliance-assessments-for-5g-wireless-networks]; national protocols (ANFR artificial-traffic generation on 3.5 GHz to capture steerable-beam exposure) [126: ANFR (Sefsouf, Conil, Agnani), "Extensive 5G measurement campaign to monitor EMF exposure in France," Comptes Rendus Physique 25, 63–73, 2024 — https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/physique/articles/10.5802/crphys.183/]. Open gap: no standardized extrapolation factor from instantaneous 5G measurements to maximum exposure (beamforming, TDD) [127: arXiv:2303.04619, "Overview of the Evaluation Methods for the Maximum EMF Exposure in 5G Networks," 2023 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04619].

Measured levels: Swiss national monitoring (2025) — median exposure <1% of the 50 V/m limit, max 0.48 V/m; school peak 0.43 V/m [128: Swisscom, "Third federal mobile radiation measurement report" (21 May 2025) — https://www.swisscom.ch/en/about/news/2025/05/21-dritter-mobilfunk-messbericht.html]. France — >13,000 in-situ measurements in direct view of 5G antennas, all below limits (28–87 V/m by band) [126]; mean total exposure rose 0.68 → 1.1 V/m over ten years (snippet-verified) [129: ANFR, "Étude de l'exposition: analyse des mesures 2023," 2024 — https://www.anfr.fr/fileadmin/medias/exposition-ondes/2025/2024_Analyse_mesures_2023.pdf]. Switzerland-wide academic studies: no overall ambient RF-EMF increase after 5G rollout despite 18× traffic growth [130: ARPANSA, "Large Swiss study indicates negligible increase in background RF-EMF exposure after 5G network rollout" (n.d.) — https://www.arpansa.gov.au/large-swiss-study-indicates-negligible-increase-background-rf-emf-exposure-after-5g-network-rollout; 131: Nature JESEE, "Spatiotemporal trends of ambient radiofrequency electromagnetic fields (2014–2023)," 2026 — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41370-026-00909-z].

### 3.4 Power lines and occupational exposure

Typical levels: directly below 380 kV overhead lines, ~4.8 µT at 1 m; above 380 kV underground cable, ~3.5 µT — below the 100 µT German public limit even at maximum line current [132: BfS, "Exposure to electric and magnetic fields from high-voltage lines," 2009 — https://www.bfs.de/EN/topics/emf/expansion-grid/basics/fieldstrain/field-strain.html]. EPRI: beneath US HV lines (115–765 kV), fields typically 10–>100 mG (1–>10 µT) [133: EPRI, "EMF and your Health" (n.d.) — https://emf.epri.com/emf-health/]. Occupational measurement practice: IEEE 644 (power-frequency fields) [64], IEC 61786-1 [65], EU Directive 2013/35/EU (occupational EMF risk assessment) [134: EUR-Lex, "Directive 2013/35/EU" (occupational EMF), 2013 — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/LSU/?uri=CELEX%3A32013L0035], NIOSH Manual 98-154 (ELF occupational procedures) [135: OSHA, "Extremely Low Frequency (ELF) Radiation: Evaluating Exposure" (n.d.) — https://www.osha.gov/elf-radiation/exposure-evaluation; 136: CDC/NIOSH, "Manual for Measuring Occupational Electric and Magnetic Field Exposures" (98-154), 1998 — https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/5254]. Quantified occupational worker-exposure magnitudes near energized equipment were not extracted from the studies identified (abstract-only) [137: IEEE, "ELF Electric and Magnetic Fields Exposure Assessment of Live-Line Workers for 132 kV Transmission Line" (n.d.) — https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/4104561; 138: Bioelectromagnetics, "Effects function analysis of ELF magnetic field exposure in the electric utility work environment," 1997 — https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/%28SICI%291521-186X%281997%2918%3A5%3C365%3A%3AAID-BEM4%3E3.0.CO%3B2-0; 139: PMC, "Acute effects of ELF electromagnetic fields: a field study of linesmen" (PMC1009855), 2023 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1009855/].

### 3.5 Lab economics

EMC: FCC Part 15B SDoC $1,500–$3,500; EN 55032+55035 CE $3,000–$6,000; multi-market $10,000–$20,000; medical IEC 60601-1-2 $8,000–$18,000; pre-compliance $800–$1,500/half-day [140: Compatible Electronics, "EMC Testing Cost Guide" (n.d.) — https://www.celectronics.com/Resources/EMC-Testing-Cost-Guide; 141: Compliance Management Group, "EMC Testing" (n.d.) — https://cmgcorp.net/services/emc-testing/]. Uncertainty reporting is mandatory under ISO/IEC 17025 [94, 140]; CISPR radiated-emissions uncertainty budgets run to ~5.2 dB [5, 140]. Common failures: radiated emissions most frequent (SMPS/clock harmonics, unfiltered I/O cables) [105].

## 4. Controversies, gaps, and disagreements

1. **FCC vs ICNIRP vs IEEE limits** — real procedural divergence (1 g vs 10 g averaging; incident vs absorbed power density >6 GHz; 6 vs 30 min averaging; 100 µT vs 200 µT 50 Hz public B-field between EU 1999/519/EC and ICNIRP 2010) [71, 72, 74, 75].
2. **EHS (electromagnetic hypersensitivity)** — real symptoms, no validated test procedure, provocation studies consistently negative; WHO reframes as IEI-EMF [142: WHO, "Electromagnetic hypersensitivity" (n.d.) — https://www.who.int/teams/environment-climate-change-and-health/radiation-and-health/non-ionizing/hypersensitivity; 143: WHO, "Fact sheet N°296: Electromagnetic Hypersensitivity" (Dec 2005, archived) — https://web.archive.org/web/20071116024800/http:/www.who.int/mediacentre/factsheets/fs296/en/index.html; 144: Rubin, Das Munshi & Wessely, "Electromagnetic Hypersensitivity: A Systematic Review of Provocation Studies," 2005 — https://www.simonwessely.com/Downloads/Publications/Other_p/103.pdf].
3. **5G measurement gap** — no standardized maximum-exposure extrapolation factor; instantaneous measurements underestimate potential exposure [127, 145: MDPI, "A Survey and Tutorial on 5G Electromagnetic Field (EMF) Measurement" (n.d.) — https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4001/7/4/91].
4. **Uplink and wearable exposure under-measured** — compliance focuses on downlink/base-station and phone-at-head scenarios; skin-adjacent wearables at 3.5/26.5 GHz concentrate SAR/PD superficially [146: Environment International, "Assessing radiofrequency electromagnetic field exposure in multiple European microenvironments," 2025 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412025002910; 147: Sensors, "Assessment of EMF Human Exposure Levels Due to Wearable Antennas at 5G Frequency Band," 2023 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9823937/; 148: PMC, "Low-Cost Sensors in 5G RF-EMF Exposure Monitoring" (PMC12845799), 2025 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12845799/].
5. **Consumer EMF meters** — no published peer-reviewed accuracy benchmark vs research instruments found; EPRI evaluation project (2023) status unverified [149: EPRI, "Evaluation of Consumer-Grade ELF Meters" (project notice, Aug 2023) — https://restservice.epri.com/publicdownload/000000003002027647/0/Product; 150: InspectAPedia, "EMF ELF Measurement Tool Accuracy & Calibration" (n.d.) — https://inspectapedia.com/emf/EMF_Measurement_Tools.php; 151: MiToMeter, "EMF Meter Accuracy — What ±6 dB Really Means" (n.d.) — https://www.mitometer.com/post/emf-meter-accuracy-what-6-db-really-means; 152: MDPI Sensors, "Instruments and Measurement Techniques to Assess Extremely Low-Frequency Electromagnetic Fields," 2025 — https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/15/4866]. Professional RF meters can specify ±6 dB accuracy (≈4× in power) — often overlooked [151].
6. **EV post-repair exposure drift** — standards do not require re-measurement after component replacement [114].
7. **Unverified/flagged items in this research round:** ICNIRP 2010 "24 h averaging" claim (unverified) [78]; IEC 62209-1 numeric uncertainty cap (PDF blocked) [24]; IEEE 1528 tilt angle value (unverified) [30]; IEC 61000-4-6 lower frequency edge (IEC 2023 says 150 kHz; some vendors say 9 kHz) [15, 16]; IEC 61000-4-8 300 A/m continuous (vendor claim, not standard Table 1) [17, 18, 19, 22]; CISPR 11:2024 numeric limit tables (PDF blocked) [83]; ICNIRP 2020 50 W/m² occupational reference level (inferred from snippets) [73: ITU (H. Mazar), "EMF, New ICNIRP Guidelines and IEEE C95.1-2019 Standard" (slides) (n.d.) — https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-d/oth/07/16/D07160000020001PDFE.pdf]; IEEE C95.1-2019 numeric limits (PDF blocked) [79] [standards-gaps; methods-gaps; applications-gaps].
