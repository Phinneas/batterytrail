## Executive summary (inference-led synthesis)

- **No company has yet shipped a commercial full-solid-state automotive battery.** As of mid-2026 the landscape is: pilot lines up, B-sample cells shipping to OEMs, one OEM road-test prototype (Mercedes EQS with Factorial quasi-solid cells, Feb 2025), one North American road-test vehicle (Stellantis Dodge Charger Daytona with Factorial, June 2026), and Chinese semi-solid packs (NIO/WeLion) that were mass-produced then discontinued for lack of demand [87][88][50][52].
- **Electrolyte routes are split three ways:** sulfide (Toyota/Idemitsu, Solid Power, SK On via Solid Power, LG, CATL all-solid, BYD, Gotion Gemstone, Nissan, Hyundai), oxide ceramic (Samsung SDI, QuantumScape's ceramic separator, ProLogium), and polymer (Blue Solutions) — plus quasi-solid/semi-solid polymer-gel hybrids (Factorial FEST, CATL condensed, WeLion, Gotion G-Yuan, SES AI) [14][19][48][79][90][44][127].
- **Claimed launch years cluster 2027–2030:** Toyota 2027–2028 [28], Samsung SDI H2 2027 [23], Nissan FY2028 [93][94], SK On 2029 [33][34], CATL/BYD small-batch 2027 [85], Gotion mass production 2030 [72], Mercedes "by end of decade" [49], Hyundai/Kia publicly skeptical ("not before 2030") [99][100].
- **The single most honest market signal:** several 2025–2026 reversals — NIO halting its 150 kWh semi-solid pack, Hyundai/Kia publicly walking back timelines, SES AI pivoting away from automotive EV cells, GM exiting its Indiana JV and the SES Chungju venture, Honda canceling its North American EV launches before partnering with QuantumScape [88][99][60][101][102][7].

## Scale-up reality: pilot lines, GWh, costs, manufacturing challenges

### Announced pilot lines / capacities (sourced numbers only)
- **QuantumScape Eagle Line** — automated pilot line at San Jose headquarters, inaugurated Feb 4, 2026; no GWh figure published; cells for OEM sampling and field testing; licensing model means VW/PowerCo does scale manufacturing [2][11][12].
- **Solid Power** — continuous sulfide-electrolyte pilot line: factory acceptance testing of all key equipment complete, commissioning targeted end 2026; SK On cell line in Korea (SAT complete April 2026) and BMW line in Germany — "cell production lines using our technology on three continents" (CEO John Van Scoter) [13][14].
- **Samsung SDI** — S-line pilot Suwon (6,500 m², since 2022); Ulsan mass-production line planned for H2 2027; 25T KRW (~$17B) 2026–2040 investment across Ulsan/Cheonan [19][23][123][124].
- **SK On** — Daejeon pilot plant 4,628 m² (completed Sept 2025); commercialization 2029 [33][35].
- **Toyota/Idemitsu** — large electrolyte pilot plant (FID Jan 2026; completion end 2027); Idemitsu Chiba lithium-sulfide plant 1,000 t/yr; METI-certified production plan [24][28][29][30].
- **Gotion** — 0.2 GWh all-solid pilot (yield >90%) → 2 GWh line design complete; materials plan 50,000 t Li2S + 100,000 t electrolyte by 2030 [71][72][75].
- **ProLogium** — Taoyuan gigafactory (operating 2024, >600,000 cells shipped); Dunkirk phase 1 0.8 GWh by 2028 [64][65][66].
- **WeLion** — ~28.2 GWh claimed total capacity (semi-solid) across four provinces [87].
- **Nissan** — Yokohama pilot line (Jan 2025); FY2028 launch [91][94].
- **BYD** — Shenzhen pilot line; ~1,000 demo vehicles in 2027 [86].
- **CATL** — small-batch all-solid production targeted 2027 [83][85].
- **China-wide (context):** reported planned solid-state capacity 345.7 GWh (2025) and 390.7 GWh (2027) per GACO report (industry estimate) [109]; SMM reports a wave of 30 GWh-class projects and a first national SSB technical standard due July 2026 [127][78].

### Cost reality (sourced estimates; wide disagreement — see Forecasts section)
- **S&P Global Mobility:** SSB costs ~$500/kWh by 2025, over 5× lithium-ion; initial use limited to premium segments [112].
- **CRU:** ASSB production cost at best 2.3× conventional Li-ion cells today; cost parity "will not be achieved in the next decade" [113].
- **TrendForce:** solid-state costs expected to fall to CNY 0.6–0.7/Wh only by 2035 [111].
- **Fastmarkets:** LFP cells at $54.63/kWh in China (April 2026, +13.6% y/y); proposes $85/kWh as proof-of-concept benchmark for solid-state scalability [114].
- **BYD:** targets cost parity with liquid Li-ion at ~$70/kWh (its own stated goal) [86].
- **Gotion:** targets 1 yuan/Wh for Gemstone [76].
- **WardsAuto citing industry:** solid-state cells currently 10–30× more expensive than conventional Li-ion [58].
- **Gotion + Factorial claims vs. reality check:** semi-solid G-Yuan is already GWh-scale in deployment per SMM [128].

### Manufacturing challenges (sourced)
- **Sulfide electrolytes** are moisture-sensitive → dry-room requirements dominate plant design (Solid Power's own pilot construction; IOPscience review on ASSB fabrication) [14][115].
- **Ceramic separators** need high-temperature sintering/heat treatment — QuantumScape's Cobra is explicitly a next-generation heat-treatment process [10].
- **Solid–solid interfacial contact, mechanical integrity, large-scale manufacturability** are the three hardest ASSB challenges; dry-electrode processing is a key enabler for sulfide systems (IOPscience review; LG; Nissan–LiCAP; Springer dry-electrode review) [38][91][115][116].
- **Thin lithium metal anodes** are a cost bottleneck (Nature Energy techno-economic assessment) [117].
- **Process simplification:** ProLogium claims core production reduced from 17 to 11 steps and "hot-flow dehydration" of 8 min vs 8 hr (self-reported, CEO interview) [131].
- Academic cost modeling shows sulfide ASSBs can approach competitiveness only if material-compatibility issues are solved and production is scaled (6 GWh scenario modeled) [116].

## Semi-solid vs. full solid-state — honesty of claims

- **Factorial FEST is quasi-solid**, not full solid-state: company's own materials say "quasi-solid electrolyte technology" — a solid matrix infused with liquid/gel-like electrolyte. Industry analyses (Electronic Design; MotorTrend) call the Mercedes EQS prototype and Stellantis Charger Daytona cells semi-solid/quasi-solid [48][53][57][125][133]. The "first solid-state EV" headlines around the EQS should be read as first quasi-solid lithium-metal road car, not full ASSB.
- **CATL "condensed battery" is a semi-solid product** — CATL's own chief scientist called it a type of semi-solid state product with condensed electrolyte; Prof. Fichtner (Helmholtz-Institut Ulm) classifies it as an "almost solid-state battery" [80][118].
- **NIO/WeLion 150 kWh is semi-solid** (gel-like polymer/oxide+polymer composite, "in-situ solidification"); NIO never claimed full solid-state [88][90].
- **Gotion G-Yuan is quasi-solid/semi-solid**, positioned as a bridge to the Gemstone all-solid line [77][128].
- **SES AI cells are semi-solid** (hybrid solid-liquid) lithium-metal [61].
- **Full solid-state claims (defensible as of mid-2026):** Solid Power (sulfide, but with LiSi anode in commercial target), Toyota/Idemitsu (sulfide), Samsung SDI (anode-less oxide-type), ProLogium (all-inorganic ceramic), Nissan (sulfide), CATL/BYD/Gotion all-solid programs (2027+), LG (sulfide R&D), Blue Solutions (polymer LMP — genuinely liquid-free, but polymer-based with operating-temperature constraints) [14][19][25][44][64][82][86][91][118].
- **QuantumScape nuance:** the separator is a solid ceramic and the cell is anode-free lithium metal, but the industry debates whether the presence of liquid in the cathode makes it "hybrid" — academic literature groups such designs under "hybrid solid-liquid electrolyte systems"; QuantumScape itself markets the QSE-5 as solid-state lithium-metal [1][9][119][120]. Flag: terminology contested; QS's own marketing calls it solid-state.
- **Bottom line:** as of mid-2026 every mass-produced "solid-state" automotive product on the road or in series production (NIO/WeLion, CATL condensed, Factorial-based prototypes) is semi-solid/quasi-solid. All-full-solid products target 2027–2030 [86][88][110][120][128].

## Forecasts & disagreements

- **IDTechEx:** "Solid-State Batteries 2026–2036" report — SSB market could reach **US$10B by 2036** [103]; earlier editions projected $9B by 2035 and $8B by 2031 — i.e., IDTechEx keeps pushing its market-size horizon outward as dates slip [104][105][106].
